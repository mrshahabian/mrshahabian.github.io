Chapter 02 · Squeezing and trading off

Codecs & the latency–quality trade-off

🗜️ Codecs (compress / decompress)
H.264 (video) + AAC (audio) — the widely compatible default with a good quality balance.
HEVC (H.265) — better for high-quality 4K/8K content, but more limited device adoption.
⚖️ Latency vs quality
🏟️ Live sport / eventslatency critical
🎬 On-demand filmquality first
ABR technologies like HLS help reduce latency, but ultra-low latency for real-time interactivity is still hard.
💡 Rule of thumb: a few seconds of delay is invisible on a movie — but if your neighbour cheers a goal before you see it, live latency has failed.
Chapter 03 · HTTP Live Streaming

How HLS works

HLS adapts media quality to changing bandwidth for smooth playback, and supports encryption, captions and alternative audio tracks. It works efficiently on Apple devices and many other platforms.

Chapter 11 · The open alternative

DASH: Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP

A widely adopted adaptive streaming protocol, similar to HLS — media is segmented into time-based chunks and quality adapts to the network. Its key difference: it's codec-agnostic.

EXAMPLE MPD (SIMPLIFIED)
{{ m.t }}
🗂️ MPD — the manifest describing tracks, bitrates and chunk durations, with finer control over switching logic and alternative content.
📶 ABR in DASH — switches between Representations seamlessly to avoid buffering.
⚡ LL-DASH — low-latency DASH for live sport: high quality with minimal delay from capture to screen.
💡 Each Representation in the MPD is the DASH equivalent of one HLS variant playlist.
