Chapter 01 · How the internet agrees on routes

BGP & peering basics

Border Gateway Protocol shares routing information between networks called Autonomous Systems. BGP peering is simply two routers connecting to exchange that information.

{{ b.icon }}
{{ b.t }}
{{ b.d }}
Role on the internet
BGP decides which path data takes to reach its destination, letting ISPs control traffic flow, choose best routes, and keep the network stable.
Why it matters in SDN
In SDN, BGP works with a central controller managing routing decisions from one place — easier to control, faster to update, more efficient.

📝 Research task — solving non-transitive routing

The non-transitive rule creates a scaling problem: connecting 100 VPCs by peering needs a full mesh of n(n−1)/2 = 4,950 connections. Research AWS Transit Gateway as the alternative.

1. Explain how Transit Gateway enables transitive routing — how A reaches C via the hub.
2. Explain how it simplifies route-table management compared with peering.
3. Prepare a single diagram or bulleted list comparing Peering (mesh) vs Transit Gateway (hub-and-spoke) topology.
