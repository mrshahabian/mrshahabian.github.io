Chapter 01 · The definition

What is edge computing?

Edge computing decentralises compute, storage and network resources — bringing processing closer to data sources and end users, and reducing reliance on centralised cloud data centres.

🏢
Infrastructure Edge

Micro data centres near last mile networks — real racks, run by service providers or enterprises.

📱
Device Edge

Smart devices with local compute — phones, IoT sensors, autonomous systems. Limited power, often mobile.

🛣️ The last mile network is the separation point between the two — remember this, it comes up in the first quiz.

Chapter 10 · Two sides of one line

Infrastructure Edge vs Device Edge

🏢
IEDC
LAST MILE
📱
DEVICE
🏢 Infrastructure Edge (IEDC)
• Micro data centres near last mile networks
• Supports low-latency, high-throughput workloads
• Typically managed by service providers or enterprises
📱 Device Edge
• Smartphones, IoT devices, autonomous systems
• Limited compute, storage and power
• Often battery-powered and mobile

Session wrap-up

Send the decision, not the data

✅ Covered

What edge computing is, its five drivers, where cloud-centric IoT breaks, and Infrastructure vs Device Edge across the last mile.

🐄 Applied

A full mist → fog → cloud walkthrough on the smart farm, with the numbers behind the 99% data reduction.

🧩 Framed

SCALE and SCANC, the edge latency ladder, and how edge sites interconnect — MMRs, IX vs EX, cloud on-ramps.

🎯 The one idea to take away

The farm does not win by having a faster uplink — it wins by deciding locally and transmitting conclusions. When you design an edge system, the question is never "how do I move this data?" but "how little of it needs to move at all?"
